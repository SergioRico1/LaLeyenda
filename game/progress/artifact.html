Asset pipeline

in progress

Bar: Every model loads at a sane size with its animation clips intact, under 300KB.

  1. Round 1

    36 models fetched from the CC0 library and optimized to .glb (9.5MB total, animations preserved). Models rendered at wildly wrong scales.

    Biggest remaining gap: Model normalization is broken: three.js, the glTF node transforms, and the GPU disagree about how big a skinned model is — the foundry rendered ~200 units instead of 5.

  2. Round 2

    Root cause found and split in two: gltf-transform's flatten/join double-applied armature transforms on skinned models, and no analytical method measures these models correctly. Pipeline now skips flatten/join for skinned documents, and sizes are calibrated from rendered pixels.

    Biggest remaining gap: Calibration running.

  3. Round 3

    Sizes calibrated by rendering each model's silhouette under an orthographic camera of known extent. Found the last cause of the scale bug: every clip re-asserts the armature's normalizing scale on frame one, so a model's size was only valid until the mixer ticked. The pipeline now drops root scale tracks; parts still animate as authored.

    Biggest remaining gap: Fourteen models remain over the 300KB budget. Voxel models carry tiny palette textures, so converting them to vertex colours should collapse those to one material each.

Island scene (builder)

in progress

Bar: blind A/B against reference/island_hero.png

  1. Round 1

    Terrain, stepped beach/grass plateaus, sand paths and the voxel water shader render correctly. Buildings blocked on the scale bug.

    Biggest remaining gap: Water reads too dark and too uniformly blue; foam is evenly scattered where the reference clusters it near the shore. No buildings visible yet.

  2. Round 2

    Terrain rebuilt to the measured recipe: island scaled to 26 cells with taller steps, beach ring stepping up to a plateau, grass plots stamped as rounded rectangles with sand between them reading as paths, sun/shade wall colours baked into vertex colours, and lighting retuned to the reference's bright low-contrast exposure.

    Biggest remaining gap: The terrain still has no surface noise at all - the reference has 25 unique colours in a patch where we have 1 - and the plots are laid out arbitrarily rather than composed.

  3. Round 3

    With the chrome fixed, the verification found the gap had moved to the world: our HUD is now the better-looking half of our own screenshot. Measured, not asserted - sea luminance std-dev 10.7 against the reference's 26.9, detail pixels 10.1% against 24.9%, and empty grass plots reading as unpainted rectangles.

    Biggest remaining gap: The amount of water is right (40.2% of frame against the reference's 43.1%). The texture is not. A round is running with those numbers as its acceptance criteria.

HUD and panels (Clash objects, Kingshot layout)

in progress

Bar: blind A/B against reference/clash/coc_hud_collect.jpg

  1. Round 1

    Clash UI language reverse-engineered into a 1600-line implementation spec (src/ui/UI_SPEC.md): full token palette, the four-layer rule that makes a surface read as Clash (constant-weight ink contour, hard gloss step at ~50%, warm top rim, dark lip with zero-offset extrusion), portrait and landscape layouts, and component specs.

    Biggest remaining gap: Nothing implemented yet - the spec exists, the HUD does not.

  2. Round 2

    HUD restructured to Kingshot's layout: four corner clusters became one bottom bar with five slots, seven separately-framed pills became one translucent capsule with dividers, building timers became dark capsules, and the sim's next-action verdict is now stated in words above the bar. Chrome hides 16.9% of the portrait screen; against Clash we win on chrome.

    Biggest remaining gap: The four-layer rule was being applied per component by hand, so each round fixed some objects and regenerated the defect in others. Now enforced by npm run audit:layers, which measures the rendered pixels.
